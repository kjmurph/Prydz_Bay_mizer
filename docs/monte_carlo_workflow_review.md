## 2. The lineage before this chain (phases 59–86)

Compressed, so the rest of the document stands alone.

**The phase-61 member protocol**, which everything up to phase 88 used *(header,
`61_members_ramped_matchgrowth.R`)*:

```
apply the catchability draw
  -> ramp initial_n toward the drawn abundance in log space,
     steady(preserve = "erepro") at each step        [K = 1 in production: one jump]
  -> cap the reproduction level at 0.9
  -> tolerance ladder 0.01 / 0.005 / 0.002 / 0.001, steady(preserve = "R_max")
  -> 118 yr unfished spin-up
  -> stability screen: CV over the last 40 yr < 0.25, trend over the first 50
```

Three decisions were settled there and have survived every subsequent change:

- **The gamma draw was dropped.** Drawn `gamma` ran 0.42×–1708× base and pushed
  110 of 190 member-species pairs over the reproduction cap before `steady()` was
  ever called — the direct cause of phase 60's `erepro` of 8111. Members now span
  **abundance and catchability uncertainty only**; physiology is internally
  consistent rather than sampled. This must be stated in the methods. *(header)*
- **`matchGrowth` does not belong in the member protocol.** Tested at two
  positions. Inside the ramp (phase 61) it produced `erepro` 1.44e18. At the
  position that looked right — after `steady(erepro)`, before the recap (phase 83)
  — on 40 members it took usable members from 16 to 2, `erepro` reached 8.4e12, and
  17 of 40 failed outright on `validParams: search_vol must not contain non-finite
  values`, because `matchGrowth` scales `search_vol` by
  `age_mat(params)/sp$age_mat` with no guard for a non-finite realised `age_mat`.
  It also did not pin growth: median absolute age deviation moved 0.0454 → 0.0326.
  It remains correct in the *reference* recalibration, where there is no
  perturbation and no `preserve = "R_max"` ladder afterwards. *(measured
  2026-08-14; phase 83 = `83_whale_scaling_arms.R`)*
- **`matchGrowth`'s default target is a placeholder.** With no `age_mat` column it
  falls back to `age_mat_vB()` computed from `k_vb`, which here is 0.2 for all eight
  air-breathing groups and 0.5 for all six LTL groups — 14 of 19 species on two
  round defaults. Targeting it moves `gamma` on the untouched, already-calibrated
  base by up to 3.15× (leopard seals). The protocol targets the base model's own
  realised `age_mat(BASE)` instead, on which `matchGrowth` is exactly a no-op
  (factor 1.000000, measured). *(header)*

**The reproduction and `R_max` work (phases 64–71, 83)**, which set up everything
in §6:

| phase | question | result | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | Can the reproduction level raise the pre-exploitation whale stock? | **No.** `setBevertonHolt` preserves the steady state by construction: at 0.9 → 0.1 it scales `R_max` by exactly 9.000 and `erepro` by 1/9, leaving `initial_n` at 0.93–1.00 and RDD at 0.90–1.00. ## 3. The chain, phase by phase

### 3.1 The ISIMIP3a 2004 window (phases 87–91) — 2026-08-14

**The defect.** The ISIMIP3a protocol permits historical catch for calibration
*"on the condition that only years up to and including 2004 are used"*; later years
are the evaluation window. The FishMIP catch reconstruction
(`calibration_catch_histsoc_1850_2004`) ends in 2004 while the effort forcing
(`effort_histsoc_1841_2010`) runs to 2010. Phase 45 took its fitting window from
*effort*, so it ran to 2010, and `coalesce(Yield_g, 0)` turned absence of data into
an observation of no catch. *(`docs/VM_RERUN_PLAN.md`)*

It was not a technicality:

- **79% of all toothfish effort falls in 2005–2010**, including its effort maximum
  of 1.000 in 2008, against a recorded catch of zero.
- With the `+1 g` offset in the log objective pricing a zero at up to 11.7 log
  units, the refit drove toothfish `q` **down 65×**, leaving modelled catch at
  **0.031 of observed** across the 33 real years.
- Toothfishes carried **26.4% of the pooled objective on 11.6% of the
  observations**. Dropping the phantom zeros changes **26 of the top 167** members —
  so cut A itself moved, and with it every figure built on it.

**The fix, and the rebuild it forced.** Fitting window is now, per species,
`first year with effort → min(2004, last reported catch, last effort year)`, with
missing catch dropped as `NA` and never coerced to zero. Because the fit changes the
ranking, the whole ensemble was rebuilt: `87_substitute_draws.R` (draws, §5) →
`88_full_ensemble.R` (1,668 member states on `params_ref_p86_agemat.rds`) →
`89_catchability_refit_2004.R` → `93_rerank_p88.R`. ~13–14 h on 30 VM cores.

**Phase 90 is the reason it can be trusted.** `90_catchability_ab_test.R` ran the
fit on 10 cached members under one rule change at a time and caught two things the
first draft of phase 89 had changed unintentionally *(header)*:

1. **The estimator.** The draft updated `q` from the ratio of *pooled sums* across
   members; phase 45 used the *median of per-member ratios* with a 50× step cap.
   A pooled sum is dominated by the largest members and landed every species below
   target (krill 0.26, toothfishes 0.29, squids 0.44, shelf 0.52) where the median
   lands five of them on 1.000. Restored; `P89_ESTIMATOR=pooled` reproduces the draft.
2. **Whale catchability was left fitted.** Phase 45 held baleen, sperm and minke at
   their drawn `q`, recorded in `45_catchability_multipliers.rds` under
   `$held_at_one` and `$note` — fields the *script* never wrote, which is how the
   decision was lost. Restored.

Validation on the top 10 usable phase-77 members, median member RMSE, lower better:

| multiplier set | RMSE |
|---|---|
| **phase 89, both corrections** | **0.9641** |
| the then-current ensemble's stored multipliers | 1.0783 |
| no correction at all (drawn `q`) | 1.1635 |
| window fix alone, whales fitted | 1.3291 |
| phase 89 first draft | 1.3623 |

**The window fix alone is worse than doing nothing** — the whale runaway swamps it.
Both corrections are needed. *(`docs/VM_RERUN_PLAN.md`)*

A third correction landed later: **fit only on members that passed.** Phase 88
writes a state for every member that converges on the ladder, but the stability and
`erepro` screens live in `88_full.rds` and do not gate the state files — so the
first run fitted on all 1,668, of which only 427 are usable. Harmless in ensemble 44
(99.2% stable, 14 members' difference); dominant in phase 88 (31.2% stable). On the
unfiltered run krill's multiplier reached 2.615e5, pinning krill `q` at the `QMAX = 1`
ceiling in 100% of members from drawn values spanning 0.002–0.822 — exactly the harm
that justified holding the whales. `P89_SCREEN` now gates it, defaulting to
`usable`. *(header, `89_catchability_refit_2004.R`)*

**Result:** 1,668 built, all converged, but **427 usable (31.2% stable)** against
ensemble 44's 1,654 (99.2%) on an identical screen and spin-up. Flagged open at the
time; §3.3 explains it.

### 3.2 The whale-recovery eliminations (phases 94, 95, 98) — 2026-08-15/16

Three experiments, all **test-only**, all retained as reviewer fallbacks. Each
asked whether a different mechanism was the constraint on whale recovery. All three
answered no, which is what pointed at mortality.

- **Phase 94** (`94_future_projection.R`) — project 1841→2100 under repeated climate
  with fishing off (`none`) and held at the 2001–2010 mean (`hold`), to distinguish
  "recovery is slow" from "the depleted state is itself an equilibrium".
  **Recovery is blocked, not slow**: 90 unfished years close under 4% of the baleen
  depletion gap and under 0.1% of sperm's. It also documents a real trap — therMizer
  does *not* error on years outside its forcing record; both `scaled_temp_effect()`
  and `plankton_forcing()` silently wrap, so projecting to 2100 against 1841–2010
  arrays would have used the *early* historical climate with no warning. The script
  extends the arrays explicitly. *(header)*
- **Phase 95** (`95_whale_scale_test.R`) — does doubling the whale scaling change the
  dynamics or only the units? Under Beverton–Holt, scaling `initial_n` and `R_max`
  by the same factor leaves `reproduction_level = RDD/R_max` invariant, so joint
  scaling *should* be a change of units. It is, near enough — and the departure is
  the finding: **×5 whales take antarctic krill to 2.4e-05 of calibrated**, which is
  what caps the whale stock at 2.8× and the catch ratio at 0.375. This is the first
  clear sight of the joint-consistency problem in §7. *(header, measured 2026-08-15)*
- **Phase 98** (`98_whale_gamma_test.R`) — raise whale `gamma` (search volume, hence
  intake and growth) 25%, applied immediately after the draws and *before* the first
  ramp `steady()`, on the grounds that applying it later is a different experiment.
  Tests whether whales are growth-limited rather than recruitment-limited. *(header)*

**What they collectively eliminated**, leaving mortality: `z0 = 0.6·w_inf^(−1/3)` —
a fish-derived allometry — gives baleen whales a biomass-weighted total mortality of
**0.00128 /yr**, a mean lifespan of **781 years** and an implied maximum age of 3,592.
With perfect compensation a depleted stock closes its gap at rate `M`, so 90 unfished
years can close at most `1 − exp(−90M)`: **baleen 10.9%, sperm 15.0%, minke 75.8%**.
That analytic bound explains phase 94's measured 3.76% / 0.06% / 5.23%, and no
reproduction parameter can beat it. The same defect explains the catch: phase-88
catch/stock over 1930–2010 is 4.9%/yr removal against a production of 0.13%/yr, a
**38× mismatch**; at literature `M ≈ 0.04` production is 4%/yr, the same order.
*(measured 2026-08-16)*

### 3.3 Phase 99 — the convergence audit, and the pivot

`99_convergence_audit.R`, read-only: builds nothing, rewrites no reference model.
This is the phase the rest of the chain follows from. *(measured 2026-08-16)*

**The pilot.** Perturb baleen ×5 on `params_ref_p86_agemat.rds` and call `steady()`
at the phase-88 ramp settings (tol 0.01, `t_max` 300, `preserve = "erepro"`):

| | |
|---|---|
| `steady()` reports convergence | **TRUE** |
| of the ×5 applied, surviving | **4.995** |
| same at tol 1e-4, `t_max` 2000 | **1.398**, and it reports non-convergence |

Two compounding mechanisms, both inside `steady()`:

1. **The multiplier is erased before the projection starts.** `steady()` sets
   `rates_funcs$RDD <- "constantRDD"` with `constant_reproduction = getRDD()` of the
   state handed to it. A 23× multiplier on `initial_n` raises RDI 23× but RDD only
   **1.106×**, because RDD saturates against the un-scaled `R_max` (level → 0.995).
   The fixed point it walks toward is ~1.1×, not 23×.
2. **It cannot walk there and cannot tell.** Convergence is `distanceMaxRelRDI` over
   `t_per = 1.5`. Baleen `M = 0.00128/yr` gives a ~780-year relaxation, so RDI moves
   ~0.2% per period — inside tol 0.01 at *any* abundance.

**The census.** 427 usable members plus the reference as member 0, 2,000 years each,
zero fishing, from each member's stored post-spin-up state:

| over 427 members | pinned forcing | protocol (cycled) forcing |
|---|---|---|
| lose ≥1 species to <1e-6 | 230 (53.9%) | **239 (56.0%)** |
| all 19 within [0.5, 2] | 14 (3.3%) | **14 (3.3%)** |

The stored 1841 state holds baleen **1.34×** and sperm **2.07× above** their own
equilibria, identical under both forcings. **The whale 1841 stock is a slowly
decaying transient, not a baseline** — and it is the denominator of every depletion
and recovery statistic. Minke is the internal control that closes the argument: its
median relaxation is 33 years, `steady()` genuinely resolves it, and it is the one
whale group that shows recovery dynamics.

**Two corrections came out of building it:**

- **`steady()` walks the climate record.** `projectToSteady` starts at `t = 0`,
  which therMizer wraps to 1841, so `steady(t_max = 5000)` cycles 1841–2010 about
  30 times. Phase 99 pins forcing for every arm and keeps an unpinned `ramp_asrun`
  arm for fidelity.
- **Repeat the ENSO window; never pin a year.** The ISIMIP3a spin-up is ctrlclim
  1961–1980 repeated six times over 1841–1960 — verified bit-for-bit in this
  model's own arrays, so **model year 1841 *is* climate year 1961**. Model-1841 sits
  0.043 °C above the cycle mean, and that alone invented a **39× minke drift** over
  2,000 years (1.099 under cycled forcing). A reading of "minke sits 39× below its
  equilibrium" was retracted on this basis. Separately, phase 88's spin-up index
  `s0@n[118, , ]` is year **1958** — `project(t_start, t_max = N)` returns `N+1`
  rows, so that is 117 elapsed years, ENSO cycle position 17 of 20.

**Why the phase-88 screen missed all of it:** half-lives here are 438–943 years,
i.e. 0.07–0.16%/yr, far inside a CV threshold of 0.25 over 40 years and a relative
trend threshold of 0.025 over 50. **The screen was looking at the wrong timescale.**

#### 3.4.1 How the mortality targets are actually applied

Five points that a methods section needs and that are easy to get wrong.

**One coefficient, not one value.** `4.22/t_max` is applied to every adjusted group,
but the resulting M differs per group because `t_max` is per-species. The coefficient
is taxon-blind by choice, following Hoenig's own recommendation that the combined
regression "be used for predictive purposes for all three groups" (fish, cetaceans,
molluscs). `P100_CONV` exposes the alternatives (`rule3`, `rule46`, `then2015`).

**Hoenig predicts from longevity, not from body mass.** There is no size term in the
relation at all — size enters only through the per-group choice of `t_max`. What the
edit *replaced* was a size allometry: mizer's default
`z0 = z0pre · w_inf^z0exp = 0.6 · w_inf^(−1/3)`, a cross-species power law in
asymptotic mass, which is what gave baleen whales `M = 0.00128/yr` and a 781-year
mean lifespan.

**External mortality is flat in size within each species.** mizer computes
`μ_ext,i(w) = z0,i + z_ext,i · w^d_i`. This model has **`z_ext = 0` for all 19
groups**, so `ext_mort()` equals `z0` at every size — verified, the row minimum and
maximum are identical for every group. Groups that were not adjusted sit exactly on
the allometric default (e.g. antarctic krill 0.3727, leopard seals 0.00783), which is
the check that the edit hit only the intended 11. This flatness is a real modelling
assumption, and it is what the phase-100 member pilot flagged as making every group
whose mortality rose more fragile.

**`z0` stores the residual, not the Hoenig total.** Because `z0` is *external*
mortality and mizer computes predation endogenously, `edit_z0()` sets
`z0 = 4.22/t_max − M_predation`, with adult predation measured on the state at zero
effort. For baleen, sperm and orca predation is ~0 so `z0` ≈ the target; for minke
the subtraction is 0.00079 and for toothfishes 0.0101 (target 0.13001 → `z0` 0.1199).
The orca kernel edit is therefore applied **before** `z0` within any arm containing
both, since it changes the predation being subtracted.

**The `z0` species-parameter column is not the deprecated `z0`.** mizer 3.1.0
deprecates the `z0` *argument* to `setExtMort()` in favour of `ext_mort`, and its own
help page says so explicitly — "Not to be confused with the species_parameter z0."
The species-parameter **column** is the documented, supported route and is what
`edit_z0()` writes (`s$z0[i] <- ...; species_params(p) <- s`). No `ext_mort` array is
ever supplied, so mizer recalculates external mortality from the species parameters,
which is the intended behaviour. As everywhere else in this project, `ext_encounter`
is saved and restored around the `species_params<-` call.

**Toothfishes is the one target that is not longevity-derived.** Its `t_max` of 32.46
is `4.22 / 0.13` — a literature M (Dunn et al. 2006, adopted by CCAMLR for the Ross
Sea) pushed back through the relation so it can enter the same code path. It is not a
maximum age and must not be reported as one, and because Dunn et al. used Hoenig
(1983) as one of three estimators the conversion is a partial round-trip rather than
an independent check. Full citation and wording in `docs/mortality_targets.md`.

**Two caveats from the source paper**, worth pre-empting rather than being asked:
Hoenig's regressions rest largely on unexploited or lightly exploited stocks, and he
notes that applying the technique to heavily exploited stocks "may result in an
underestimate of the mortality rate"; and the Addendum records Ricker's suggestion of
a geometric-mean regression instead, which for the combined line would put baleen at
0.0420 rather than 0.0469 — about 10% lower.
- **The orca kernel**: `beta` 0.5579 → 45.7 (Tucker & Rogers 2014), `sigma` 2.5 → 2.0.
  The old `beta` is faithfully derived from `csvs/predator_parameters_updated.csv`,
  but that row's **source cell is empty** and it puts orca's preferred prey at
  **19 tonnes** — heavier than the orca. `sigma` 2.5 was an orphaned hand-tune set
  alongside a `beta` of 500 that was later reverted.
- **Small-diver `beta`** 293.8 → 960 (geometric rather than arithmetic mean of member
  ratios).
- **Interaction edits** from `csvs/interaction_edits_v1.csv`.
- **The out-of-domain subsidy realigned** by an odds-ratio solve,
  `k_i = [p_i/(1−p_i)]/[s_i/(1−s_i)]`, applied as a scalar on each species'
  `ext_encounter` row. It is a fixed point, not a one-shot, and **must run after the
  ladder as well as before** (`P100_SUB_POST`) — running it only before leaves orca
  at 0.47 of its intended share. With the post-ladder pass all nine groups land
  within **2%**.

**What is deliberately not in it, all measured:**

| lever | result |
|---|---|
| `w_min` → birth mass | **worse**, max `erepro` 21.8 → 41.1. mizer has no parental care, so pups die before maturing; weaning mass is the correct input. Retained in `ARM_DEF` as `wmin` specifically as a documented negative result. |
| `w_mat` 0.65–0.80 + `matchGrowth` | worse on every blocked group, and newly pushed minke over 1 |
| interaction ceiling (non-zero → 1) | helps (0.648 → 0.413 alone) but is a **bounding test**, not a parameterisation |
| mortality targets for leopard seals / large divers / small divers | **unreachable** |

**Why those three groups keep allometric mortality.** `erepro = 2·w_min·RDI/E_R`.
The 95.4× gap between leopard seals (0.393) and medium divers (0.0041) is **17.9×
from `w_min`**, 1.92× from RDI/biomass, 2.73× from inverse E_R/biomass — `w_mat` is
not the driver. Part is an aggregation inconsistency (medium divers took the
*minimum* weaning mass across 8 taxa, king penguin 10 kg, rather than the mean of
52.9 kg); the rest is a real structural limit, since small divers' `w_min`/`w_max`
of 0.49 is biologically correct for penguins and `erepro ≤ 1` then caps reproductive
throughput. Satisfying `erepro < 1` would need maximum ages of ~38–50, ~95–120 and
**384** years.

**The single-arm attribution** (`101_pilot_*`, 20 members through the full phase-88
protocol on each arm) — *run every single-edit arm; do not infer ownership from a
combined arm*:

| arm | stable | admissible | usable | `erepro ≥ 1` breaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | 15 | 16 | 13 | small divers 2, flying birds 1, toothfish 1 |
| **z0** | 15 | **8** | 6 | **leopard seals 8**, minke 3, small divers 3 |
| **orca** | 14 | **18** | 12 | none |
| **subsidy** | 15 | **18** | 14 | small divers 2 |
| z0_orca | 16 | 8 | 7 | leopard seals 9, minke 3, small divers 3 |
| all | 16 | 6 | 5 | leopard seals 13, small divers 4, minke 3 |

**Both the orca kernel and the subsidy edit beat the control.** The whale `z0` edit
is what collapses admissibility, and it does so **through leopard seals**, in the arm
where the orca kernel is unchanged. The root cause predates every edit: leopard
seals (`erepro` 0.393) and small divers (0.648) have almost no reproductive headroom
in the reference model, so any perturbation raising their mortality tips them over —
which is why they also supply the control arm's own breaches. *(measured 2026-08-16)*

**And this forced the protocol rebuild.** On the phase-88 protocol, the new
reference gave **2/20 usable** against the p86 control's **13/20** — the `max erepro`
headroom of 0.8436 vs 0.6481 is exactly what the member protocol consumes.

### 3.5 Phases 103–104 — the member protocol rebuilt

`103_rmax_placement_and_recap.R` answered the two questions the replacement could
not be written without; `104_members_rebuilt.R` is the replacement. Both are covered
in detail in §6. In summary, phase 104 changes **five** things and holds everything
else — the draws, the catchability, `K = 1`, `matchGrowth` off, the tolerance ladder
and its `preserve = "R_max"`, and the stability thresholds:

1. `R_max` scaled **at the ramp** with `initial_n`, never postcap, and never both.

### Why each rule

- **Mesozooplankton is never drawn down.** It takes 79% of its encounter from the
  prescribed resource, which is identical across members, so a member-specific
  reduction is an artefact of applying one random draw to all 19 groups. `U(1, 10)`
  is **conservative against the draws themselves**, whose >1 median is 11.19 and
  upper quartile 60.5. *(header, phase 87)*
- **Whales are drawn up, on a different rationale, and the methods must say so
  separately.** Baleen, minke and sperm were direct targets of extensive
  exploitation, so the hypothesis is that pre-exploitation populations were much
  larger than the contemporary-calibrated reference. This is not the mesozooplankton
  argument and should not be presented as one rule.
- **Orca is excluded at phase 104.** It was not exploited to the extent the other
  three were, so there is no case for lifting its pre-exploitation stock. **This is
  the clear win of the phase-104 draws**: admissibility 5 → 11 of 20 and minke drift
  0.037 → 0.907. Orca had been in the `U(5, 50)` substitution for 1,270 of 1,668
  phase-88 members, and minke collapses under it (orca at 43× explains minke's
  realised biomass at ρ = −0.96 while minke's own draw explains nothing).
- **REPLACE, not conditional, for the whales.** Substituting only draws below 1
  leaves each whale a *mixture* of substituted values and its own original draws
  that were already ≥ 1, and the mixture differs by species. Under `U(5, 100)`
  conditional that gave baleen a median of 22.8 against minke's 39.9 — **baleen
  lifted least despite being the most heavily whaled**, an artefact of the original
  prior rather than a choice. Replacing every draw gives all three the same
  distribution (medians 15.0 / 14.8 / 15.2, max exactly 25). Under conditional
  substitution minke reached **1.67e5** in the generated file.
- **REPLACE, not multiply.** A multiplicative rule would be unbounded above —
  krill's largest draw of 75.8 would reach 758. Replacement matches how phase 87
  already treated mesozooplankton and the whales, and it bounds the result.
- **Species are selected by NAME, not position**, so a reordering upstream cannot
  silently shift which groups are treated.

### What the methods must state plainly

- **The substitution share is large.** In phase 87, draws below 1 and therefore

## 6. The re-steady and the reproduction parameters

This is the change that most needs explaining in the manuscript, because it moved a
number that had quietly been doing scientific work while looking like a numerical
setting.

### 6.1 What the re-steady is for

Each ensemble member starts from the calibrated reference model and multiplies each
group's abundance by that member's drawn multiplier. That multiplied spectrum **is
the hypothesis**: this is what the pre-exploitation (1841) ocean might have looked
like. But it is not a self-consistent state — growth, predation mortality and
recruitment no longer balance at those abundances. So `steady()` is called to walk
the model to the nearest state where they do. That is the "re-steady", and the state
it returns is the member's 1841 baseline: the denominator of every depletion and
recovery statistic in the paper.

### 6.2 The reproduction machinery, in one paragraph

Recruitment in mizer is Beverton–Holt:

```
RDD = R_max · RDI / (R_max + RDI)
```

`RDI` is egg production, proportional to spawning stock biomass and to the
reproductive efficiency `erepro`. `R_max` is the recruitment ceiling. The
**reproduction level** is `RDD / R_max` — how close realised recruitment sits to its
ceiling. Near 0, recruitment tracks the stock one-for-one. Near 1, recruitment is
pinned at `R_max` and barely responds to the stock at all.

### 6.3 What went wrong in the old protocol

**(a) The ceiling was not scaled with the draw, so the re-steady erased the draw.**
Multiply `initial_n` by 20 and `RDI` rises 20×, but `R_max` stays where the reference
calibration put it. Recruitment therefore saturates: `RDD` rises only **1.106×**. And
`steady()` *freezes* `RDD` — it sets `rates_funcs$RDD <- "constantRDD"` at the value
of the state handed to it — so the fixed point it walks toward is that ~1.1×, not the
20× that was drawn.

Measured on the phase-61 ensemble, the slope of log(realised biomass ratio) on
log(drawn multiplier), where 1.0 would be full transmission:

```
toothfishes −0.15   salps 0.02   shelf fish 0.03   squids 0.03   mesozoo 0.05
bathypelagic 0.05   mesopelagic 0.05   birds 0.07   small divers 0.13
other krill 0.13    other macrozoo 0.19   antarctic krill 0.23
leopard seals 0.40  large divers 0.47  medium divers 0.57  minke 0.60
orca 0.69   baleen 0.87   sperm 0.90
```

**For 12 of 19 groups less than 20% of the drawn variation survived into the
member.** The ensemble was far narrower in abundance space than the draws intended.
*(measured 2026-08-13)*

**(b) The reproduction level was pinned at 0.9 for a numerical reason, and that
became a scientific assumption.** Because the draw drove the level toward 1 (members
arrived pre-cap at sperm 0.983, baleen 0.948, minke 0.924), the protocol capped it at
0.9 with `setBevertonHolt(reproduction_level = 0.9)` to keep `erepro` admissible.
A level of 0.9 means whale recruitment is 90% saturated: whaling cannot deplete the
population much, and a depleted population cannot rebuild much. **That is the
reproduction clamp the entire whale-response story sat behind.** The 0.9 was a patch
for a symptom created by (a).

**(c) The half-fix had its own ordering trap.** Scaling `R_max` by the same
multiplier is the obvious remedy, but `setBevertonHolt(reproduction_level = L)` sets
`R_max = RDD/L`, so **any `R_max` set before a recap is destroyed by it**. Phase 67
scaled before the recap, got whale reproduction levels pinned at exactly 0.90 with no
change in recruitment, and drew a null conclusion about a step that never took
effect. Ordering it after the recap (`postcap`) is what phase 88 shipped.

**(d) And `steady()` could not tell it had failed.** At the ramp settings (tol 0.01,
`t_max` 300) a ×5 baleen perturbation returns **4.78 of 5** and reports **convergence**
— and that state then collapses to **0.21 over 200 years**. The convergence test is a
relative change in RDI over 1.5 years, and baleen's relaxation time on the old
reference was ~780 years, so RDI moved ~0.2% per period: inside the tolerance at any
abundance. The `postcap` states drifted **1.21–1.75×** over 200 years. They were not
steady states.

### 6.4 What changed

Phase 103 measured the four possible placements on full 19-species member draws, at
the fixed ramp budget. Whale groups, medians *(header, `103_rmax_placement_and_recap.R`)*:

| placement | realised/drawn | reproduction level | `R_max` × | drift over 200 yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **postcap** (phase 88) | **0.30–0.63** | 0.25–0.51 | 9–32 | **1.21–1.75** |
| **ramp** (adopted) | **1.05–1.29** | 0.90 | 8.7–31 | **1.004–1.008** |
| ramp + postcap | 6.1–9.8 | 0.25–0.54 | **84–916** | 1.01–1.03 |
| ramp, no recap | 1.004 | 0.9030 | 5.028 | 1.0090 |

The five changes, and the reasoning for each:

**1. Scale `R_max` with `initial_n`, at the ramp, before the re-steady.** Multiplying
both by the same factor is a **scale transformation**: `RDD` scales with them and
`RDD/R_max` — the reproduction level — is unchanged. `steady()`'s frozen `RDD` now
sees the raised ceiling, so the fixed point it walks toward *is* the drawn abundance.
Realised/drawn goes 0.30–0.63 → **1.05–1.29**, and the ramp `steady()` converged on
**every** member where `postcap` converged on half.

**2. Never scale it twice.** `ramp + postcap` **squares** the multiplier — baleen
`R_max` ×249 against the ramp's ×15.9 (and on the clean ×5 probe, ×25.14 = 5²
exactly). Phase 104 scales once, at the ramp, and says so in a comment at the call site.

**3. Give `steady()` a budget it can actually converge in** — tol **0.001**,
`t_max` **1000**, against the old 0.01 / 300. At the new settings the ×5 baleen probe
returns 1.106 and holds at 0.9917 over 200 years. tol 1e-4 / `t_max` 3000 returns
1.093 and 1.0052, i.e. no better for 3× the cost. **Cost: 2.5× the runtime, and it is
the single cheapest fix available to the protocol.**

**4. The cap stops being a patch and becomes a drawn parameter.** With the ceiling
scaled correctly the reproduction level lands **exactly at the cap by construction**
for every member — under `ramp`, the recap is the only thing setting it (`ramp` and
`ramp_norecap` agreed to four significant figures because a correct ramp already
leaves the level at the cap). So instead of inheriting 0.9, phase 104 **draws** it:

```
RECAP ~ U(0.1, 0.9), one draw per member, seed 20260816
applied as pmin(reproduction_level, RECAP)
through a NAMED vector covering baleen, sperm, minke and orca only
```

The named vector matters: groups already below their draw keep their own value, and
the other 15 groups are untouched. It maps to Beverton–Holt steepness as
`h = 0.2/(1 − 0.8L)` — L 0.1 → h 0.217, 0.5 → 0.333, 0.9 → 0.714.

The sweep that justified drawing it rather than fixing it (3 members, 1841–2100,
depletion relative to the contemporaneous unfished arm) *(header)*:

| `RECAP` | baleen depletion / recovered | sperm | minke |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.90 | 0.954 / 97.3% | 0.915 / 98.3% | 0.694 / 100% |
| 0.75 | 0.919 / 95.6% | 0.857 / 95.5% | 0.615 / 102% |
| 0.50 | 0.824 / 95.8% | 0.722 / 81.2% | 0.648 / 99.5% |
| 0.25 | 0.682 / 64.6% | 0.574 / 46.4% | 0.489 / −0.3% |
| 0.10 | 0.577 / 27.1% | 0.528 / 0.1% | 0.473 / 51.1% |

Across the range, baleen depletion spans 0.58–0.95 and recovery 27–97%. **The
parameter governs the result and is genuinely unconstrained, so it is sampled rather
than chosen.** (Caveats: three members only, and the minke column is non-monotonic —
either noise or bistability. Do not read it as a curve.)

**5. Stop trusting `steady()`'s own report — measure drift.** Each member's
post-spin-up state is projected `DRIFT_YEARS = 200` with zero effort under the
repeated ENSO cycle, and the per-species end/start ratio must lie within **[0.5, 2]**.
`steady()`'s convergence report is *not* evidence: it reported success in every
failing case above.

**And the spin-up was fixed** to `t_max = 120` **indexing the last row**.
`project(t_start = 1841, t_max = N)` returns `N+1` rows, so phase 88's `s0@n[118, , ]`
was year 1958 — 117 elapsed years, ENSO cycle position 17 of 20. 120 elapsed years is
exactly six ISIMIP3a cycles and lands on position 0, seamless into model-1841.
Magnitude is small (cycle sd is 0.04 °C); it is a compliance fix that was free in a
rebuild.

### 6.5 The resulting protocol

```
apply the drawn catchability
  -> scale initial_n AND R_max by the same drawn multiplier         [the ramp]
  -> steady(tol = 0.001, t_max = 1000, preserve = "erepro")
  -> setBevertonHolt(reproduction_level = pmin(level, RECAP))  whales only, named
  -> [NO postcap R_max scaling -- that would square the multiplier]
  -> tolerance ladder 0.01 / 0.005 / 0.002 / 0.001,
     steady(preserve = "R_max", t_max = 1500)                       keep the tightest rung that converged
  -> 120 yr unfished spin-up, index the LAST row
  -> stability screen (CV over the last 40 yr < 0.25, trend over the first 50)
  -> drift screen (200 yr, zero effort, cycled forcing, all species in [0.5, 2])
```

Admissibility is `erepro < 1` for all 19 groups. Usable = **stable AND drift-ok AND
admissible**. Note that this is a stricter definition than phase 88's, and several
downstream scripts had the old *pre-drift* definition hardwired — see §7.

### 6.6 The assumptions this carries

State these in the methods; they are what a reviewer will press on.

1. **Scaling `R_max` with the draw asserts the multiplier is a hypothesis about the
   size of the system, not about density dependence.** The recruitment ceiling scales
   with the stock, so the Beverton–Holt curve is re-scaled rather than re-shaped.
   Without it the draw is a statement about standing stock alone, and the re-steady
   erases it.

2. **`preserve = "erepro"` at the ramp is not arbitrary.** `erepro` has no physical
   ceiling and can absorb draws spanning 0.5×–24,800×; making `R_max` the free
   variable (`preserve = "R_max"` at the ramp) drove `erepro` to **2,850** on real
   draws, 0/10 admissible. `preserve = "R_max"` is then correct in the *ladder*,
   after the scaling and the recap, so the ladder does not undo them.

3. **The reproduction level cannot move the unfished equilibrium.** `setBevertonHolt`
   is built to preserve the steady state: at 0.9 → 0.1 it scales `R_max` by exactly
   9.000 and `erepro` by 1/9 while `initial_n` moves 0.93–1.00 and RDD moves
   0.90–1.00. An unfished equilibrium requires recruitment to balance mortality at the
   same stock, so it must. **`RECAP` is not a stock lever.** What raised the
   pre-exploitation whale stock is scaling `R_max` *without* the compensating
   `erepro` change and then re-equilibrating.

4. **What `RECAP` controls is the trade-off, and the direction is fixed.** A *lower*
   level means recruitment is more sensitive to spawning stock, so depletion is
   deeper *and* recovery is less complete. At level 0.9 a 90% stock reduction still
   yields 52% of pristine recruitment; at 0.1 it yields 11%.

5. **Low reproduction levels are the unusual end, not the realistic one** — the
   opposite of the natural intuition. Steepness has a hard floor at 0.2 and most
   assessed stocks sit at 0.5–0.9, which maps to levels of roughly 0.5–0.97. But high
   levels give only ~5% baleen depletion against a historical >90%, so there is a
   real tension, and lowering the level purely to buy depletion would have been
   masking a stock-limitation problem rather than fixing it. The `q ≤ 10` refit
   (§3.6) is what actually resolved it.

6. **`RECAP` is a propagated uncertainty, not a fitted parameter — say so.** The
   yield objective is blind to whales: a 7.7× change in baleen catch moves the yield
   RMSE 0.3%. The ranking therefore passes a `U(0.1, 0.9)` draw through almost
   untouched, and the ensemble median is the *prior* median. Confirmed on the
   finished ensemble: **KS D = 0.076, p = 0.267** between usable and rejected members;
   median 0.464 usable against 0.495 rejected, q10–q90 0.159–0.766 against
   0.169–0.822. **Never claim it was fitted.**

7. **The whale clamp is released, not removed.** Depletion on the current ensemble is
   set mainly by drawn catchability (ρ = −0.833 with the baleen catch ratio), with
   `RECAP` at 0.366 and the abundance draw at −0.078.

8. **The member state is now demonstrably stationary over 200 years, which the
   phase-88 states were not.** That is a claim about the drift screen, not about
   `steady()`: `steady()`'s convergence report is used to reject failures, never as
   evidence of stationarity.

## 7. Open items

- **The draws are not jointly consistent, and this is the biggest one.** Antarctic
  krill still fails the drift screen in 15 of 20 pilot members (median drift 0.002).
  The draws scale predators and prey independently — whales at 15× eat the krill down
  — which is the phase-95 mechanism (×5 whales took krill to 2.4e-05) arriving through
  the ordinary draws rather than a deliberate multiplier. Protecting krill from being
  drawn *down* does not save it. Three options remain deferred: a shared
  ecosystem-scale factor with smaller per-species deviations; an explicit
  predator:prey constraint; or a much larger draw pool with a low acceptance rate.
  *(measured 2026-08-16)*

- **The screen undoes the mid-trophic prior widening.** Drawn abundance, usable vs
  rejected medians *(measured 2026-08-17)*:

  | species | usable | rejected | ratio | KS p |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | other macrozooplankton | 1.00 | 3.29 | **0.304** | 1.8e-40 |
  | squids | 0.50 | 1.51 | **0.332** | 6.0e-21 |
  | leopard seals | 0.50 | 1.21 | 0.413 | 3.2e-03 |
  | toothfishes | 0.50 | 1.13 | 0.443 | 5.3e-03 |
  | medium divers | 0.598 | 1.32 | 0.453 | 9.5e-10 |
  | large divers | 0.576 | 1.04 | 0.552 | 1.3e-03 |
  | antarctic krill | 4.91 | 6.54 | 0.751 | 1.5e-11 |

  The LTL split lifts 50% of these groups to `U(2, 10)` and the screen removes exactly
  those members. **The ensemble is reportable on the whale and reproduction axes but
  is not a fair sample of the mid-trophic abundance prior**; any statement about
  squid, macrozooplankton, diver, seal or toothfish abundance uncertainty must say it
  is conditioned on stability. The whale prior, by contrast, largely survives — minke
  14.8 vs 14.8 (p = 0.15), sperm 15.4 vs 14.9 (p = 0.40), orca 0.50 vs 0.50
  (p = 0.75); only baleen is selected, and mildly (12.7 vs 15.2, p = 7e-4).

- **203 usable of 1,668 attempted (12%).** Comparable in size to the published 167,
  but the acceptance rate is the visible face of the joint-consistency problem.

- **The drift bound [0.5, 2] over 200 years is a chosen threshold**, not one derived
  or signed off. Krill at 0.002 fails any sane bound, but the *count* of failures
  across other species is sensitive to it.

- **`80_yield_by_species_p104q10.rds` still carries the pre-drift `usable`
  definition** (265 members, not 203). The figure is correct because
  `81_yield_facets_figure.R` filters via `P81_CUTS_RDS`, but the file must not be
  reused directly without filtering.

- **`104_full_states/` lives only on the VM** (~300 MB, 1,419 states). Anything that
  rebuilds figures from member states must run there or pull them across.

- **The trophic-level subsidy defect is unresolved.** `getTrophicLevel()` is already
  known to be broken under therMizer; separately, `ext_encounter` counts as trophic
  level 0, so the out-of-domain subsidy drags computed trophic levels down (orca reads
  1.86 on p86). Work in progress in `Reference model diet assessment/`, uncommitted.

- **Whale catchability is a claim about the fishery.** Median fitted `q` of 0.964
  (baleen) and 1.78 (sperm), i.e. F near 1–1.8 /yr at peak effort. Defensible for
  industrial whaling, but it belongs in the methods as a stated assumption.
